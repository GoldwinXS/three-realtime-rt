What each feature costs

every switch in the demo panel, priced in milliseconds and frames per second — measured, per scene.

This page is a report, not a benchmark: nothing here is measured in your browser. The numbers are the committed output of the repo's quality campaign (quality-campaign/cost-matrix.json), a staircase sweep that starts from one baseline configuration and changes one thing at a time, timing each variant with GPU fences and comparing its image against a converged full-resolution reference of the same pose.

Read every row as “what turning this feature ON does”: a positive frame time is what it costs you, a negative one is what it gives back. Feature cost is strongly scene-dependent — that is exactly why there are three tables rather than one average.

Three things the numbers say that the names do not

inversion 1

ReSTIR has a negative price

restir is filed under “noise reduction”, but in a scene with more than one light it is the single biggest speed feature in the library. Switching it off makes the museum 34% slower and Littlest Tokyo 39% slower, because one reservoir visibility ray replaces one shadow ray per light. It is also more accurate. There is no scene in this campaign where restir: false was the better choice — do not ship it.

inversion 2

ReSTIR GI is a speed feature

restirGI carries an experimental label about its accuracy, which reads like something you would pay for. On the heaviest scene here it is 27% faster (47.8 → 34.9 ms) and slightly more accurate (rmse −0.12), because it replaces the lighting pass's inline GI trace with a reservoir the denoiser resolves. The saving scales with GI ray cost: it is worth −14% on 86 triangles and −27% on 141k.

inversion 3

Half-rate GI is nearly free

giHalfRate traces the indirect bounce on alternating checkerboard parity, so it buys back 10–22% of the whole frame. The accuracy column is the surprise: ±0.3 rmse, a wash, because temporal accumulation converges to the same answer either way. On a static scene it is the cheapest 20% you will ever find; the price is paid only in how fast GI re-converges while the camera moves.

Per scene

Sorted by frame-time cost. fps on → off is the frame rate with the feature on, then with it off, at that scene's baseline. accuracy is the change in RMSE against the converged reference when the feature is ON — negative means the feature makes the image closer to the reference.

The zero that is not a zero

absorptionShadows (“tinted shadows”) reads as 0.00 ms in all three tables above. That is true and it is not the whole truth: none of the three measured scenes contains a material that actually absorbs, and with nothing absorbing the compiled lighting megakernel is byte-identical to a build without the feature. The zero is the cost of carrying the feature, and it is a real result — most renderers cannot say that.

The moment one material does absorb, the same switch replaces a bounded, early-outing any-hit shadow traversal with an unbounded ordered closest-hit march, and the README's measurement of the museum with its glass revealed puts that at +13.8 ms with ReSTIR on and +28.7 ms with ReSTIR off (the honest column — it is the configuration in which coloured shadows actually reach a primary surface). Different scene state, different number, same feature: budget it against the state you will ship, not against this page's zero.
